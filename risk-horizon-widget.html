What beta measures and what it earns

Beta measures how strongly a stock’s return follows the market’s. Build a stock below, see the beta it produces, the return investors require for it, and why volatility alone is the wrong measure of risk.

1. How strongly does the stock follow the market?

The economy is equally likely to be strong or weak. The market returns +47% in a strong economy and −25% in a weak one. Choose the stock’s return in each case.

Each dot is one state of the economy (red: weak, green: strong). The slope of the line through them is beta. The dashed line is the market itself, with a beta of 1.

2. What return does that beta require?

Investors require the risk-free rate plus beta times the market risk premium. The expected market return is 11%, the average of +47% and −25%.

The security market line: the required return rises in step with beta. A beta of 0 earns the risk-free rate, and the market itself has a beta of 1.

3. Volatility is not beta

Add firm-specific risk, which has nothing to do with the economy. Total volatility rises, but beta and the required return do not. In this economy the market’s volatility is 36%.

KEY INSIGHT

Stylized economy from the slides: strong and weak economies are equally likely, the market returns +47% or −25% (an expected return of 11% and a volatility of 36%), and the risk-free rate is adjustable. Beta = (stock return in a strong economy − in a weak one) / (47% − (−25%)). Required return = risk-free rate + beta × (11% − risk-free rate).
